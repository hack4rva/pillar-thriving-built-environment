# Executive Summary

The City of Richmond's Department of Public Works (DPW) deploys approximately 240 employees and 75 trucks during snow events, working in 12-hour shifts to clear over 2,500 lane miles of roadway. Publicly available information does not specify how many of these trucks are equipped with active GPS/AVL units, nor does it name a specific fleet telematics vendor or confirm the existence of an ArcGIS-compatible API. While the city provides machine-readable GIS data for street sweeping zones via its open data portal, snow priority routes are primarily available through a public-facing ArcGIS web map and a PDF document, with no direct download link for a shapefile or GeoJSON found. Operationally, DPW supervisors assess route completion in real-time, but the specific tools used (e.g., digital dashboard, radio) are not detailed. Residents are kept informed of progress through the city's official website, social media channels, and local news outlets. There is no evidence of a city-hosted public-facing plow tracker; for state-maintained roads, residents are directed to the Virginia Department of Transportation's (VDOT) tracker. The city's 311 system does not take individual snow removal requests and appears to operate from static policy information rather than real-time operational data.

# Distinction From Vdot Operations

The responsibility for snow removal in the Richmond area is divided between the City of Richmond Department of Public Works (DPW) and the Virginia Department of Transportation (VDOT). The Richmond DPW is responsible for clearing the city's 2,500 lane miles of roadways, which are categorized into Priority 1 (major arterials), Priority 2 (connector streets), and Priority 3 (local neighborhood streets). VDOT is responsible for state-maintained roads, such as interstates. This division is highlighted by the existence of a separate VDOT-hosted public plow tracker map, which the city directs residents to for information on state routes, while the city manages communications for its own streets.

# Guidance For Residents

The official guidance for Richmond residents during snow events is to understand and follow the city's established snow removal plan, which is based on a three-priority system. Priority 1 routes, including major arterial roadways and bridges, are cleared first. Priority 2 connector streets are next, followed by Priority 3 local neighborhood streets. The city explicitly states that it does not accept individual snow removal requests through the RVA311 system or any other platform, as crews are required to follow the set priority list to address the most urgent community needs first. Residents are advised to follow the city's official website and social media, as well as local news, for progress updates and timelines.
